# Real photos: public wichtounet v2 dataset (200 photos)

Generated by `python scripts/real_photos_report.py` from `outputs/e2e/e2e_real_wichtounet_cnn.csv` (config `configs/e2e_real_wichtounet_cnn.json`, CNN `cnn_v1` with temperature, tau = 0.5, maxsat_unique time limit 10 s). Every number below is computed by the script; nothing is typed by hand. Data: `data/real/README.md` (source, licence, citation). Nothing was trained, tuned or chosen on these photos: the CNN, tau, the solver settings and the vision check were all fixed before the run.

**Not independent samples.** The 200 photos show 76 distinct puzzles (test split: 40 photos, 28 puzzles; 26 of the test photos show a puzzle that is also in a train photo). The Wilson intervals treat photos as independent and are too narrow; the *puzzle bootstrap* columns resample puzzles (10,000 resamples, seed 0) and are the honest ones.

**Vision check (no verified corners).** A photo counts as a vision failure if the grid is not found, or if the CNN's empty / has-a-digit pattern disagrees with the labels on >= 12 of the 81 cells (`occ_disagree`). The threshold was fixed from synthetic test_final before this run: there, all 1,598 images with correct corners had 0 such cells, while two different puzzles differ on 24-81 cells (median 38); 12 is the midpoint of 0 and 24. A misread digit on the right cell does not count. Section 3 shows how the real photos fall around the threshold and cross-checks against the third-party outlines.

Consistency check: maxsat_weighted re-solved from the saved ProbGrids reproduces the CSV's `correct` on 200 of 200 photos where a grid was found.

Rule: >= 12 disagreeing cells or no grid = vision failure (**3 of 200 photos**; test split: 1 of 40). *outline error* = mean distance between our detected corners and the third-party outline (`outline_corners`, unverified), as % of the outline's mean side length; it is only a cross-check. Photos with a grid found and 1-23 disagreeing cells are the ones where the rule could matter; their count is in the table.

## 8. Published results on this dataset (context only, not comparable)

The dataset README lists: Wicht & Hennebert (SoCPaR 2014), Hough transform + deep belief network, **12.5% error rate on v1** (the older 160-photo version); and Wicht's Ph.D. thesis (unpublished when the README was written), **17.5% on v2** and 7.5% on the `mixed` version. These are listed only as context. We did not check the exact definition of their error rate in the paper or thesis (e.g. per photo or per cell, recognition only or with solving) or how their systems were trained, and the 12.5% is on a different version. Our numbers measure full-solution correctness of a CNN trained only on synthetic renders, so **no direct comparison is claimed**.
